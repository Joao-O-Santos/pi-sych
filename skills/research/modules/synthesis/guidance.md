# Synthesis

Separate verified metadata, source claims, inference, and missing evidence.
Inspect local project sources first when they are relevant. A local literature
index may aid relevance and provenance, but inspect its underlying source before
a precise claim; an index does not prove complete metadata, text, or coverage.
Model background knowledge is not a retrieved source.

Use the best available capability for each retrieval step rather than assuming
one fixed research path. Scholarly metadata services can expand or reconcile a
candidate set; peer-reviewed gateways can retrieve focused literature; PDF or
full-text readers can establish what a source actually says; and targeted web
fetch can verify a known publisher, DOI, correction, repository, or documentation
page. Broader search is useful when the source location is still unknown. These
roles may be provided directly to the supervisor or through an explicitly
assigned research worker; choose according to task and context rather than a
universal delegation default.

Treat tool answers as inputs to the next research decision. Reuse verified
identifiers, follow promising references, narrow or broaden queries when returned
coverage warrants it, and stop repeating a service once its marginal value is
low. Report failed or unavailable retrieval and do not silently substitute an
unverified summary for inaccessible primary material.

Organize synthesis by proposition, not source chronology. Compare methods,
populations, measures, scope, and timing before mapping agreement,
disagreement, serious alternatives, and gaps. For a supplied source set, state
that boundary. For retrieval, retain the routed search frame and stopping rule;
stop when further search mostly repeats low-relevance material rather than
claiming completeness.

For each consequential source record verified title, authors, year, DOI or URL,
type, relevant claim, method or basis, strength, and caveats. Do not invent
consensus, quotations, bibliographic fields, access, or retrieval. Source notes
are not manuscript prose; hand off evidence and uncertainty before drafting.
