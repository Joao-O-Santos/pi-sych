# Synthesis

Separate verified metadata, source claims, inference, and missing evidence.
Inspect local project sources first when relevant. A local literature index may
aid relevance and provenance, but inspect its underlying source before a precise
claim; an index does not prove complete metadata, text, or coverage. Model
background knowledge is not a retrieved source.

Organize synthesis by proposition, not source chronology. Compare methods,
populations, measures, scope, and timing before mapping agreement,
disagreement, serious alternatives, and gaps. For a supplied source set, state
that boundary. For retrieved material, retain the search frame and stopping
rationale rather than claiming completeness.

For each consequential source record the verified identity, relevant claim,
method or basis, strength, and caveats. Distinguish discovery from inspected
support and report failed or unavailable retrieval. Do not silently substitute
an unverified summary for inaccessible primary material. Source notes are not
manuscript prose; hand off evidence and uncertainty before drafting.
