# Synthesis

Separate verified metadata, source claims, inference, and missing evidence.
Inspect local project sources first. A local literature index may aid relevance
and provenance, but inspect its underlying source before a precise claim; an
index does not prove complete metadata, text, or coverage. For external
retrieval, dispatch an explicitly assigned remote-research worker with only the
needed MCP services. Report failed or unavailable retrieval. Model background
knowledge is not a retrieved source.

Organize synthesis by proposition, not source chronology. Compare methods,
populations, measures, scope, and timing before mapping agreement,
disagreement, serious alternatives, and gaps. For a supplied source set, state
that boundary. For retrieval, retain the routed search frame and stopping rule;
stop when further search mostly repeats low-relevance material rather than
claiming completeness.

For each consequential source record verified title, authors, year, DOI or
URL, type, relevant claim, method or basis, strength, and caveats. Do not invent
consensus, quotations, bibliographic fields, access, or retrieval. Source notes
are not manuscript prose; hand off evidence and uncertainty before drafting.
