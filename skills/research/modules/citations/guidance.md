# Citations

Verify bibliographic identity and the version actually inspected: author,
title, date, locator, DOI or URL, and material correction or retraction status.
Quote only wording inspected in that version. Identify whether each citation is
evidential, contextual, methodological, or illustrative; correct form does not
establish claim support.

Use available capabilities according to what must be verified. A scholarly
metadata service such as OpenAlex or an equivalent can help establish identity,
DOI, authorship, date, venue, references, or citation relationships. A scholarly
gateway can help retrieve candidate literature. A targeted fetch/browser read
can verify a known DOI, publisher, repository, correction, or retraction page.
A PDF/full-text reader should be used when exact wording, surrounding context,
methods, results, limitations, or quotations matter. Named services are
examples, not required providers.

Let one verification step inform the next. A DOI discovered in metadata can seed
a source lookup; a correction notice can change which version must be read; a
reference list can identify the primary source behind a secondary citation. Do
not repeat a generic query across every tool or treat agreement among metadata
services as proof that the cited source supports the claim.

Project `STYLE.md` or venue rules control citation system, narrative or
parenthetical form, punctuation, DOI presentation, bibliography formatting,
and local citation commands.
