# Retrospective

Propose; do not mutate. Separate observed events and actual verification from
interpretation. Prefer local lessons; require repeated evidence or an obvious
structural defect before proposing a general rule. Do not mine unattended
transcripts, create global memory, edit package state, or commit automatically.

For each candidate record:

- observed evidence, including commands or artifacts;
- repetition or structural basis and an alternative explanation;
- the smallest candidate lesson and targeted component;
- the predicted observable behavioral effect;
- behaviors that could regress if the proposal is adopted;
- held-in evaluation on the motivating cases and held-out evaluation on cases
  not used to derive the lesson;
- the proposed local target, or none; and
- the human decision: accept, modify, defer, or reject.

A held-in improvement does not establish generalization. Preserve previously
good behavior in held-out and regression cases, and leave every durable change
to human review.
