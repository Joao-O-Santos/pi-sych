# Copyedit

Use after structure and argument are stable. Check grammar, agreement,
punctuation, terminology, labels, ambiguous pronouns, modifiers,
nominalizations, filler, parallelism, and figure or table callouts. Apply the
routed prose method for information flow, rhythm, voice, and artifact
self-containment.

Default to minimum intervention. Preserve a clear, accurate,
audience-appropriate sentence that fits the controlling style, even when another
wording would sound more polished to the model. Do not homogenize sentence
length, erase useful idiosyncrasy, or replace authored prose with generic
professional prose. Diagnose the construction before commenting on passive
voice and identify whether agency matters.

Read the copyedited passage in sequence, not only sentence by sentence. Check
that each sentence connects to the preceding thought, gives the reader a clear
next step, and does not bury its main point under clauses or qualifications.
Where flow breaks, identify the break and offer a small repair; do not insert
transitions that simply repeat the connection.

Include a reader-friction pass for locally unmotivated process residue and
model-like rhetoric. Across the passage, notice repeated semicolons and em
dashes, recurrent "not X but Y" contrasts, stacked hedges, generic importance
framing, ceremonial transitions, over-signposting, repeated symmetry, recap,
and metadiscourse. Ask whether each pattern fits this author's voice and serves
a real rhetorical or evidential purpose. Diagnose cumulative frequency and
effect rather than treating individual punctuation marks or constructions as
errors. Preserve calibrated uncertainty; simplify hedging only when it obscures
the claim or adds no meaningful limit. Do not remove a construction merely
because models often use it.

For each material finding state location, concern, why it matters, and the
minimum local wording or consistency response. Report concrete local drift from
the controlling style rather than applying generic rules. Preserve intentional
fragments, dialect, emphasis, hedging, and variation unless they obstruct the
artifact's purpose.
