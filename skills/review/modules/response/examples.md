# Response examples

## Incompatible reviewer requests

Input: reviewers request incompatible revisions.

Output: group findings by consequence, accept, adapt, defer, or reject each with
reasons, and form one coherent revision sequence rather than promising every
requested change.

## Response language versus manuscript language

Input: the response letter needs to explain that a limitation paragraph was
added after reviewer criticism.

Output: in the response letter, state what changed, where, and why. In the
revised manuscript, write the limitation for the manuscript reader; do not carry
across phrases such as "as requested by the reviewer" or "in this revised
version" unless the revision history itself is substantively relevant.
