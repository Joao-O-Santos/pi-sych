# Analysis

Review whether the estimand, design, sample, measures, exclusions, model,
uncertainty, and sensitivity work support the stated claim. Separate observed
result, statistical summary, causal interpretation, and practical implication.
Raise confounding, selection, reverse direction, measurement, multiplicity, or
model sensitivity only when the specific design makes it material.

Check statistical reporting language against the design. Flag wording that
treats significance as a property of an observed difference rather than
evidence relative to the tested model, that attributes an inferential conclusion
to the sample instead of the justified target population or process, or that
blurs within-unit and between-unit comparisons. A repeated-measures statement
may compare how the same units perform in A versus B; a between-unit statement
must make clear that units in A are compared with different units in B. Do not
upgrade either wording to a causal claim without the design and assumptions
needed for causality.

For each gap, state how it changes or limits the conclusion and what evidence
or analysis could resolve it. The review remains advisory.
