# Quantitative

State the estimand, design, assumptions, diagnostics, transformations,
missing-data and exclusion rules, multiplicity choices, and claim limits.
Distinguish confirmatory, exploratory, and sensitivity analyses. Inspect failures
and unexpected values rather than silently filtering them. Report effect sizes,
uncertainty, denominators, and practical interpretation; significance is neither
causation nor importance, and non-significance is not no effect.

Treat statistical significance as a statement about **evidence relative to the
tested model**, not as a property newly acquired by an observed difference. The
sample difference is descriptive whether or not a threshold is crossed. When
the design and sampling support inference beyond the observed units, phrase the
inferential conclusion at the target-population or target-process level rather
than saying that "the sample differed significantly". When they do not support
that generalization, narrow the claim explicitly.

Make comparative wording reflect the design. For a within-unit or repeated-
measures contrast, wording such as "people performed better in A than in B" is
appropriate because the same people contribute to both conditions. For a
between-unit contrast, write "people in A performed better than people in B",
or the domain-equivalent wording, so the sentence does not imply that the same
units were observed under both conditions. Prefer "assigned to A/B" when
randomized assignment is substantively relevant. Reserve causal wording for
designs and assumptions that support it.

Keep versioned inputs, code, outputs, tables, figures, and prose traceable. Test
model assumptions and alternatives proportionately; state when evidence cannot
support a causal interpretation. Never report expected or unexecuted output as a
result.
