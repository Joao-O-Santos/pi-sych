# Qualitative

Match the research question to a stated approach: thematic, grounded-theory, discourse, content,
narrative, or case analysis make different claims. Explain sampling logic, adequacy or information
power, recruitment boundaries, and why cases can answer this question; do not translate saturation
into a universal threshold.

Develop and document codes inductively, deductively, or in combination. Preserve a codebook or
analytic memos, revisions, excerpts, and decision trail. State reflexive and positional influences;
seek negative or deviant cases and plausible alternative readings. Use triangulation or inter-coder
processes only when they fit the approach, and report what they did and did not establish.

Select quotations for analytic function, not decoration; de-identify material and protect
confidentiality. Distinguish participants' accounts, analytic interpretation, and contextual claims.
Describe transferability through setting, sample, and boundaries rather than statistical
generalization. An audit trail supports interpretability; it does not make findings objective or
causal.
