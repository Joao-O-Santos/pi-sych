# Reporting

## Answer the research question

Start with the group, organism, outcome, or process the reader cares about.
Follow the finding with the estimate, uncertainty, and relevant test. Supply
units and identify the scale of the estimate. Use past tense for observed
outcomes and present tense for warranted inferences about the population or
process. Give the study conditions when they delimit the inference.

- **Slope:** name the direction and what one unit of the predictor means for
  the mean response. For an unstandardized slope, report *b*, its units and
  *SE* or an interval. A slope across subjects compares subjects.
- **Groups:** give the comparison in the order used by the contrast. Keep
  each reported mean with its uncertainty. Use adjusted/model-estimated
  means when they answer the question; identify them as such.
- **Interaction:** test the difference between effects directly, then use
  simple effects or trends to explain the pattern. State the multiplicity
  adjustment once for the relevant set of comparisons.
- **Significance:** when the decision is useful, say that the evidence is
  statistically significant. For an inconclusive test, report the estimate
  and uncertainty; a failure to detect a difference does not establish equality.

## Give the design and interpretation their own jobs

Methods describe what researchers sampled, allocated and measured, including
which unit received the intervention and when. Describe model fitting where
it helps reproduce the analysis. For a teaching simulation, label any
hypothetical protocol where it is introduced and keep it consistent with the
data and analysis.

In Discussion, use the space for a consequence, a plausible explanation,
or a limitation specific to the study. A well-scoped Result need not end
with a lesson about statistical inference. Include technical detail where
readers need it to interpret or reproduce the result.

Check sample definitions, labels, units, scales, denominators, estimates,
rounding, and contrast signs against the code and output. Match captions and
text to what their tables and figures show. See the [worked reporting
examples](examples.md).
