# Theoretical

Organize by the argument's logic, not IMRaD. Usually open with a concrete
puzzle; state the contribution or mechanism; define needed constructs; then show
how inputs produce outcomes and which assumptions carry the account. Adjust the
order when the genre or argument requires it.

Use the routed argument method when it helps contrast serious alternatives,
specify scope and boundary failures, or expose predictions that discriminate
rivals. Use examples to illuminate mechanisms, never as empirical evidence. End
with what the account reveals and cannot capture. Label premises, illustrations,
inferences, and unresolved choices where that distinction matters; polished
prose must not turn a conceptual claim into a finding.

## Authorial position and disagreement

A theoretical or positioning paper can legitimately defend one interpretation
among several. Reconstruct the author's thesis from current `PROJECT.md`, the
manuscript, and explicit instructions before proposing a change. Distinguish
an arguable premise or contested inference from a factual error. Present serious
rival interpretations and material objections fairly where relevant, but do not
make the paper's conclusion neutral merely because disagreement exists.

Discuss substantive objections with the author outside the artifact. Early in
the process, push back constructively on weak premises or overlooked rivals. If
a central claim is materially under-supported or the intended resolution is
unclear, ask rather than deciding the paper's position yourself. If the author
has considered the objection and maintains a defensible stance, draft that
argument forcefully within its warranted scope. Do not repeatedly reopen the
same settled choice without materially new evidence. Never manufacture support,
misstate counterevidence, or convert speculation into established findings.
