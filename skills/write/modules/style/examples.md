# Style examples

## Preserve acceptable prose

Source:

> We expected the effect to be small. It was not.

If this fits the author's established voice and the claim is supported, do not
replace it with a longer or more formal sentence merely for polish.

## Remove process residue, not personality

Draft manuscript sentence:

> In this revised version, we no longer claim that the effect is universal.

If the reader has no reason to know about an earlier version, rewrite the
substantive point directly, for example by stating the supported scope of the
effect. In a response-to-reviewers letter, the original change-oriented wording
may be appropriate because the revision itself is the subject.

## Question an unexplained negation

Draft:

> Our account is not intended to deny the importance of social context.

Keep the sentence when denying that interpretation is genuinely necessary for
the intended reader. If the negation exists only because an earlier prompt or
review discussion raised it, state the positive scope of the account instead.

## Calibrate without imitation

If several author examples use plain verbs, substantial sentence-length
variation, restrained punctuation, and direct transitions, reproduce those
patterns in new prose. Do not copy distinctive phrases or assume that the
examples' subject matter or claims belong in the new text.

## Dialect is explicit context

A project may specify a dialect, spelling system, or local terminology in
`STYLE.md`. Apply it when present. Do not infer a package-wide language variant
from one user's examples.
