# Argument analysis

Use this method to expose how a conclusion depends on premises, assumptions,
inference, and scope. Do not reduce skeptical review to searching for named
fallacies.

## Map the argument

**Default:** Record separately:

1. the conclusion and its scope;
2. stated premises;
3. the inference from premises to conclusion;
4. unstated assumptions needed by that inference;
5. alternative explanations or rival accounts;
6. observations that would discriminate rivals;
7. any logical or inferential defect; and
8. the consequence of each defect for the conclusion.

**Reason:** Different weaknesses require different repairs. A premise can be
poorly supported even when the inference form is valid, and a valid inference
can remain underdetermined by a rival account.

**Exceptions:** A short operational decision may need only its controlling
premise and consequence. Expand the map only when the omitted distinction could
change the judgment.

**Review test:** Can a reader identify which premise or assumption carries each
inferential step and how far the conclusion generalizes?

**Failure mode:** Rephrasing the conclusion as a premise, inventing hidden
assumptions without textual basis, or producing an exhaustive map that obscures
the material issue.

## Classify the problem

**Default:** Use the narrowest accurate category:

- a named logical fallacy concerns the relation between premises and
  conclusion;
- an alternative explanation concerns underdetermination by another account;
- weak evidence concerns support for a premise;
- a missing scope condition concerns generalization beyond supported cases;
- selection, measurement, reverse direction, common cause, multiplicity, and
  model sensitivity are specific threats when the claim depends on them.

Explain how the issue weakens, narrows, or leaves unresolved the conclusion.
State what observation, evidence, or analysis would discriminate serious
rivals.

**Reason:** Accurate classification prevents a plausible alternative from
being misrepresented as proof of invalid logic and prevents a fallacy label
from substituting for analysis.

**Exceptions:** Several categories may interact. Keep them separate before
explaining their combined consequence.

**Review test:** Does the named category identify the actual relation at fault,
and does the proposed repair address that relation?

**Failure mode:** Superficial fallacy detection, treating an alternative as
disproof, or saying only that "more evidence" is needed without naming what
would discriminate accounts.
