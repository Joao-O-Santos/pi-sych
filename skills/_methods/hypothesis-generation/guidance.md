# Hypothesis generation

Use this method to generate candidate explanations from an observation,
anomaly, conflict, puzzle, null, or deviant case. These heuristics are
generative aids, not evidence: they can produce candidate hypotheses but cannot
make those hypotheses evidentially supported.

## Generate a portfolio

**Default:** Apply a small, relevant subset of these generative
transformations, then state a discriminating implication for each retained
candidate:

- ask whether the contrary is partly true;
- reverse the proposed causal direction or add a common cause;
- test extreme and zero conditions;
- propose moderators, populations, boundaries, or time scales;
- decompose the cause, mechanism, or outcome;
- vary the operationalization or representation;
- generate multiple mechanisms before preferring one;
- identify counterforces that could produce the observed net pattern;
- inspect null, deviant, and boundary cases;
- reconcile conflicting findings through scope, mechanism, or measurement;
- borrow an analogy and state exactly where it breaks; and
- use bounded qualitative, quantitative, or simulated probes to clarify which
  observations could distinguish candidates.

**Reason:** Transforming the problem produces genuinely different mechanisms
and boundary conditions rather than cosmetic restatements of one favored
account.

**Exceptions:** A well-constrained task may need only one serious rival or a
clarification of the existing account. Stop when new transformations merely
repeat prior candidates.

**Review test:** Do the candidates differ in mechanism, scope, or predicted
observation, and is there an observation that would discriminate them?

**Failure mode:** Treating a long list as theoretical progress, preferring the
most attractive story without support, or using post-hoc flexibility to
explain every possible result.

## Preserve epistemic status

**Default:** Label the source of each candidate, the timing of its generation,
and whether it is exploratory, preregistered, or previously supported. Preserve
nulls, counterexamples, and unresolved tensions. Record what evidence would
support, weaken, or discriminate the candidate.

**Reason:** The history of generation changes what can be claimed from the same
data and prevents a deviant result from disappearing into a retrospective
story.

**Exceptions:** Early conceptual work may have no formal preregistration, but
it can still distinguish observed material, generated conjecture, and prior
support.

**Review test:** Could a reader tell what was observed, what was generated from
it, and what independent support remains necessary?

**Failure mode:** Presenting an exploratory explanation as predicted evidence,
rationalizing away a null, or treating analogy and plausibility as support.
