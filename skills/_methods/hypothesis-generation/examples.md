# Hypothesis-generation examples

## From anomaly to rivals

Observation:

> The effect appears in the morning sample but not the evening sample.

Candidate portfolio:

1. a time-of-day moderator changes the mechanism;
2. the samples differ on an unmeasured common cause;
3. the measure has different reliability across sessions;
4. opposing fatigue and practice effects cancel in the evening.

Discriminating implications should follow. Repeating the study at both times
with balanced assignment and a reliability check separates these candidates
better than another unstructured replication.

## Preserve a deviant result

Bad:

> The null reveals the moderator predicted by our theory.

Better:

> The unexpected null suggested an exploratory moderator account. Retain the
> null, label the account post-hoc, and test its discriminating prediction in
> independent data.

Why:

The null generated the candidate; it did not independently support it.

## Analogy

An analogy to ecological competition may suggest counterforces in an
organization. State which entities, resources, and interactions correspond,
then identify where intentional human coordination breaks the analogy.
