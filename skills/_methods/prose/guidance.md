# Prose

Use this method for substantial prose and for diagnostic review. Explicit
user instructions, accepted project style, venue requirements, and artifact
needs override package defaults. Preserve evidence, uncertainty, citations,
placeholders, dialect, and purposeful variation.

## Paragraph jobs

**Default:** Give an ordinary expository paragraph a discernible opening
move, development through reasoning or evidence, and a consequence or
transition. Build a topic-sentence outline before drafting when the argument
is unclear. Build a reverse outline before major revision: state what each
existing paragraph actually does, then reorder, merge, cut, or split.

**Reason:** One identifiable job helps readers connect each sentence to the
argument and helps writers find duplication, drift, and missing moves.

**Exceptions:** Introductory, transitional, serial, dialogic, and deliberately
fragmentary paragraphs may use other structures.

**Review test:** Can a reader identify the paragraph's job, and does each
sentence advance that job?

**Failure mode:** Mechanical topic sentences and concluding restatements that
produce repetitive school-essay prose.

## Information flow and revision

**Default:** Use familiar-to-new information flow when that supports topic
continuity. Place the main action early when delay increases reader memory
load, and use end-weight for longer or more complex material. Prefer concrete
verbs and nouns. Revise in this order when feasible: argument, section jobs,
paragraph logic, information flow, sentences, then words.

**Reason:** Readers understand new material more easily when its relation to
established material is visible; global repair also prevents polishing prose
that should be moved or removed.

**Exceptions:** Suspense, contrast, rhythm, technical definitions, and genre
conventions may justify another order.

**Review test:** Does each sentence begin from an intelligible point of contact
and place its most consequential new material where readers can absorb it?

**Failure mode:** Enforcing one sentence shape, stripping rhythm, replacing
precise terminology merely to sound concrete, or adding ceremonial openings,
repeated summaries, and generic importance claims.

## Register and model defaults

**Default:** Unless audience or genre requires formality, use a natural,
capable professional register rather than institutional or essay-like prose.
Prefer common, audience-appropriate words while retaining precise technical
terms. Unpack ambiguous noun stacks and unnecessary nominalizations; do not
ban either construction. Keep hedges that represent real uncertainty, but
remove defensive fog and unsupported intensifiers.

Treat punctuation and rhetorical templates as choices, not polish. Use em
dashes sparingly rather than as the default hinge for qualifications. Avoid
repeated `not X but Y` contrasts, binary or three-part symmetry, ceremonial
openings, repeated conclusions, and metadiscourse that narrates the document
instead of advancing it.

**Exceptions:** A real contrast, useful rhythm, necessary navigation, formal
setting, or established authorial voice may require any of these forms.

**Review test:** Does the prose sound like a knowledgeable person addressing
this audience, or like a generic model performing sophistication?

**Failure mode:** Making prose uniformly casual, deleting necessary hedging,
flattening deliberate rhythm, or replacing one mechanical style with another.

## Voice and agency

**Default:** Choose active or passive voice from topic continuity, information
focus, and responsibility. Before commenting, diagnose a genuine grammatical
passive: look for a passive auxiliary, usually a form of *be* or *get*, with a
past participle and a corresponding active relation. A form of *be* alone is
not passive. Retain passive voice when the acted-on entity should remain the
topic or the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or already established. Prefer
active voice when agency or responsibility matters. Never mechanically replace
passive voice.

**Reason:** Voice organizes attention; identifying agency can also be part of
the substantive claim.

**Exceptions:** Venue rules, deliberate ambiguity, rhythm, or disciplinary
conventions may control a local choice, but they do not change the grammatical
diagnosis.

**Review test:** What is the sentence's grammatical voice, what should remain
the topic, and would naming the actor materially improve accuracy or
responsibility?

**Failure mode:** Treating every *be* construction as passive, applying an
active-voice quota, or preserving agentless prose that conceals who acted.
